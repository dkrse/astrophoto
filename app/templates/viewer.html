🎯 Plate solve

Finds the stars in the catalogue (ASTAP, local star database) and measures the real image scale – from it the focal length, field of view, centre and rotation.

⬇ Annotated JPEG
ResultSky & photometry Objects
?
Result – what the values mean
Focal length
Real focal length = 206.265 × pixel (µm) / scale. Differs from the nominal one by reducers / flatteners and backfocus: within ±2 % normal · 2–5 % check the spacing or the value in the equipment · > 5 % wrong reducer / pixel size / binning. “Use … mm” stores the measured value in the telescope.
Image scale
Arcseconds per pixel, measured from the star positions (exact, unlike the nominal one). Sensor scale is shown separately when the image was resized / drizzled.
Field of view
Sky area of this image (width × height).
Centre
RA / Dec (J2000) of the image centre – use it to re-frame the same target next night (NINA / ASIAIR “Framing”, plate-solve sync).
Rotation
Camera angle, measured from north towards east. Keep it the same between nights to stack / mosaic without wasted edges; 180° difference is common after a meridian flip and does not matter.
Pointing
Where the project object lies: ✓ within 15 % of the way to the corner = centred, ⚠ off-centre, ✗ not in the frame.
Image size
Note when the image is cropped / resized compared with the sensor (the focal length is corrected then).
?
Sky & photometry – what the values mean

Measured only on linear raw light frames (not JPEG / stacks). Star brightnesses are matched with the Gaia catalogue (BP band).

Limiting magnitude
Faintest stars detected with the given SNR in this single exposure. Higher = deeper. Depends on exposure, aperture, sky and transparency – compare frames with the same settings.
Sky brightness
Like an SQM meter, mag/arcsec² – higher = darker sky: ≥ 21.5 dark site (Bortle 1–3) · 20.5–21.5 rural (Bortle 4) · 19.5–20.5 suburban (Bortle 5–6) · < 19.5 city (Bortle 7–9). The Moon, twilight and narrowband filters change it a lot. Needs a bias / dark (camera offset), otherwise estimated.
Zero point
Magnitude of a star giving 1 ADU per second. Same equipment + filter + gain → it should be constant; lower by 0.2–0.5 mag = haze / thin cloud, dew on the optics or low altitude. Used for the transparency between nights.
Photometric quality
From the stars matched with Gaia (SNR ≥ 20, not saturated): scatter – robust spread of measured − catalogue (≤ 0.05 precise, ≤ 0.12 normal, more = clouds, gradient, trailing, narrowband filter) · zero point ± – its uncertainty · linearity – slope of measured vs catalogue magnitude, 1.00 ± 0.05 linear (else stretched data, wrong offset / bias) · saturation – stars brighter than this are clipped · field – zero point change edge to edge (> 0.15 mag: dew, gradient, obstruction) and corners vs centre (vignetting, normal without flats).

Objects in the field

Deep-sky objects from OpenNGC (NGC, IC, Messier) and Stellarium (Sharpless, Barnard, LBN, LDN, van den Bergh, RCW, Cederblad, Collinder, Trumpler, Arp, HCG, PK …) that lie in the solved frame, then the brightest stars from HYG (HD number; proper name / Bayer–Flamsteed designation).

Type
Object class; for stars the spectral type (O B A F G K M, hot → cool) – the coloured dot is the star colour from the B–V index. var = variable star with its catalogued range.
Mag
Total brightness (lower = brighter): V for stars and most objects. Galaxies fainter than ~13 mag need long integration.
Size / dist.
Apparent size (major × minor axis) – compare with the field of view – and the distance in light years where it is known (stars: Hipparcos / Gaia parallax).
Measured
Brightness of the star in this frame (zero-point calibrated, Gaia BP band) and the difference Δ to the Gaia BP catalogue value. |Δ| ≤ 0.1 normal · larger blend, variable star or a colour difference · sat. saturated (too bright for this exposure – it measures too faint). V (Mag column) and BP differ by the star colour.

🔬 Optics inspector

Measures size (FWHM) and shape (elongation, direction) of the stars in every part of the frame. Best on a single sharp light frame with many stars. Only a summary is stored (for session values and trends).

SummaryField map Diagnosis Optics & sampling
?
Summary – what the numbers mean
Stars measured
Usable stars found (galaxies, blends, hot pixels dropped). Below ~50 the values are less reliable; min. 15.
FWHM
Star diameter at half its peak brightness – the main sharpness value. In ″ (needs a plate solve): < 2″ excellent · 2–3″ good · 3–4″ average · > 4″ poor (typical amateur site 2–4″).
HFR
Half-flux radius ≈ FWHM / 2. The number NINA, ASIAIR, SharpCap show while focusing – lower = better focus.
Elongation
Long / short axis of the stars. < 1.15 round · 1.15–1.3 slightly stretched · > 1.3 stretched (guiding, wind, tilt, coma).
Seeing ≈
Atmospheric blur estimated from the centre of the field (with the optics' diffraction removed). < 2″ good · 2–3″ average · > 3″ poor. If the stars are much larger than the seeing can explain, focus / optics is the more likely cause.
Sampling
Pixels across one FWHM. 1.5–3.5 px ideal, below = undersampled (square stars), above = oversampled (binning gives more signal without losing detail).
Field of view
Sky area of the frame – from the plate solve if available, otherwise nominal from telescope + camera.
Colour bar
green OK · orange worth checking · red problem.

Values are medians over all measured stars. Details per part of the frame are in Field map.

Diagnosis

Automatic findings from the field map, in plain language. ✓ fine, ⚠ worth checking, ✗ clear problem.

The findings describe this one frame – confirm a problem on a few frames from different parts of the night before changing the setup (wind or a guiding glitch can affect a single frame).

For tilt and curvature use a frame that is in focus; a defocused frame exaggerates both.

?
Optics & sampling – what the numbers mean
Image scale
Arcseconds per pixel = 206.265 × pixel (µm) / focal length (mm). Typical deep-sky 0.7–2.5 ″/px.
Pixels per FWHM
< 1.5 undersampled · 1.5–3.5 ideal (Nyquist ≈ 2–3) · > 3.5 oversampled. With bad seeing a long focal length is always oversampled – binning (or 2× downsample in processing) recovers SNR.
FWHM / HFR
Star size; HFR ≈ FWHM / 2 for a Gaussian star. Use HFR to compare with NINA / ASIAIR autofocus values.
Eccentricity
Other notation of elongation (PixInsight SubframeSelector): < 0.42 excellent · < 0.5 round to the eye · 0.5–0.6 borderline · > 0.6 visibly stretched. Elongation 1.15 ≈ 0.5, 1.3 ≈ 0.64.
Airy FWHM
Smallest star the aperture can form (diffraction, 550 nm) = 116 / aperture (mm) ″. A 100 mm lens: 1.2″, 200 mm: 0.6″.
Dawes limit
Closest double star separable visually = 116 / D ″. For imaging the seeing almost always dominates.
Measured vs. limit
Measured FWHM / Airy FWHM. < 2× close to the optics' limit (great seeing, small aperture) · 2–5× typical · > 5× seeing, focus or guiding limit the image, not the telescope.
Focal ratio
Focal length / aperture. Faster (lower f/) = more signal per pixel, but a much narrower focus zone.
Critical focus zone
CFZ = 4.88 · λ · N². How far the sensor may be from the perfect focus with no visible blur. E.g. f/4 ≈ 43 µm, f/5 ≈ 67 µm, f/7 ≈ 132 µm, f/10 ≈ 270 µm. Tilt across the sensor larger than the CFZ shows as one side soft; a focuser step / temperature drift should be well below it.

Diffraction and critical focus zone for green light (550 nm). Without a plate solve the scale is the nominal one from the telescope + camera.

Field map – how to read the tiles

The frame is split into 3×3 parts; each tile shows a crop from the middle of that part, the values are the median of all stars in that ninth.

Caption
FWHM px / ″ · elongation (stars). Colour: green = sharpest part, red = worst part of this frame (relative, not absolute).
Line
Direction in which stars are stretched – drawn only when elongation > 1.1 and the direction is consistent.
Typical patterns
• all tiles stretched the same way → guiding / tracking / wind
• corners stretched radially (pointing to the centre) → coma / backfocus of the corrector wrong
• one side larger FWHM than the other → sensor tilt
• all corners larger than the centre → field curvature (flattener missing / spacing)
• centre larger than the corners → focus set off-centre or spacing too long
Spread
Corner FWHM within +10 % of the centre is very good, +10–25 % acceptable, > 25 % visible in the image.
